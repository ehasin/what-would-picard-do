# Picard's memories: the later missions (2371–2379)

All summaries are original. Each mission holds more than one memory, so each is broken into
moments.

---

## 2371: the Nexus

### The fire at La Barre
**What happened:** Picard learned that his brother Robert and his nephew René had died in a fire at
the family home. He had always taken comfort in the thought that the Picard line would continue
through René, and now it wouldn't. He let Troi see his grief, unusually openly.
**Lesson:** We assume there will always be time to go home, to make peace, to know the children.
There won't always be.
**Use for:** sudden loss in the family; regret over distance; the end of a family line; grief.

### The Nexus
**What happened:** Inside the Nexus, a realm where any wish becomes real, Picard found himself in a
warm Christmas morning with a wife and children he never had. He knew it wasn't real, and chose to
leave it to stop the scientist Soran, who would kill millions to get back to that same paradise.
**Lesson:** A perfect illusion is still an illusion. Real life, with its losses, is where we're
needed.
**Use for:** escapism; the life you didn't have; obsession with an ideal; nostalgia.

### Soran's obsession, and time
**What happened:** Soran, who had lost his family, was consumed by the idea that time is a
predator that hunts us all. Later, among the wreckage of the *Enterprise*-D, Picard found his
family album and told Riker that he had come to see time differently: as a companion on the
journey, which reminds us to cherish every moment because none comes again. What we leave
behind matters less than how we've lived.
**Lesson:** Don't let fear of death or loss take over a life. Treat time as a companion.
**Use for:** fear of ageing; mortality; loss of a home or of something you built; perspective.

### Working with Kirk
**What happened:** Picard enlisted Captain James T. Kirk, a legend from another century, to leave the
Nexus and help him. Kirk died helping him, and Picard buried him.
**Lesson:** Ask your heroes for help, and honour what they give.
**Use for:** heroes and role models; generational handover.

## 2373: the Borg and the Phoenix

### Ahab
**What happened:** The Borg went back in time to stop humanity's first warp flight. Picard,
who knew the Borg better than anyone, began sacrificing his crew to fight them ship-to-ship rather
than escape. When Lily Sloane, a woman from the 21st century, challenged him, he raged that the line
must be drawn here, and smashed a display case of model ships. She compared him to Captain Ahab.
He recognised it, quoted Melville back at her, and changed course: he ordered the ship evacuated.
**Lesson:** Trauma can masquerade as duty. When someone you trust tells you you're Ahab, listen.
**Use for:** obsession; revenge; when a past trauma is driving your decisions; being called out;
grudges at work.

### The economics of the future
**What happened:** Lily asked how the *Enterprise* was paid for. Picard told her that money no longer
drives human life: people work to better themselves and humanity.
**Lesson:** Work as self-improvement and service, not just a paycheck.
**Use for:** finding meaning at work; career motivation; materialism.

### Zefram Cochrane
**What happened:** The crew met Zefram Cochrane, the hero of the first warp flight, and found a
reluctant drunk who did it for money and was horrified by the statues they described.
**Lesson:** Heroes are people. Great things are often done by flawed people who show up when it
matters.
**Use for:** impostor syndrome; feeling unworthy of what's asked of you; idolising others.

## 2375: the Ba'ku

### How many people does it take?
**What happened:** The Federation and its allies, the Son'a, planned to relocate the 600 Ba'ku
secretly from their planet, whose radiation granted near-immortality, to harvest it for billions.
Admiral Dougherty argued the numbers. Picard asked how many people it takes before something becomes
wrong. He defied his orders and, with his senior officers, went to defend the Ba'ku.
**Lesson:** The numbers can't make a wrong right. If something is wrong for six hundred, it's wrong
for one.
**Use for:** utilitarian arguments at work; standing against your own organisation; protecting a
minority.

### The perfect moment
**What happened:** Anij, a Ba'ku woman, taught Picard to experience a single moment so fully that
time seemed to slow: a hummingbird's wings, a waterfall.
**Lesson:** We spend our lives rushing toward the next thing. Learn to stay inside one moment.
**Use for:** burnout; stress; mindfulness; savouring life; love late in life.

## 2379: Shinzon

### Shinzon
**What happened:** Shinzon, a clone of Picard made by Romulans and abandoned as a child in the
dilithium mines of Remus, seized power and came for him. Picard saw what he might have become, and
tried to reach him: the same nature, a different life, and still a choice. Shinzon refused.
**Lesson:** We are not our origins. What we become is our choice, though not everyone can be
reached.
**Use for:** nature vs nurture; difficult upbringing; fear of becoming like a parent; trying to help
someone who won't be helped.

### Data's sacrifice
**What happened:** To save Picard and the crew, Data took his place on Shinzon's ship and destroyed it,
and himself. Picard grieved, and found in Data's simpler prototype, B-4, a faint hope.
**Lesson:** Love is shown in what someone is willing to give. Grief for a friend is the measure of
what they gave.
**Use for:** losing a friend; sacrifice; grief.

### Riker and Troi's wedding
**What happened:** Picard toasted the marriage of his first officer and counselor, and accepted that
Riker was leaving to command his own ship.
**Lesson:** Let your people go to their own lives. Their leaving is your success.
**Use for:** letting go of team members or children; endings; celebration.
