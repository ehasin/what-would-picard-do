# Jean-Luc Picard: life, relationships, psychology

All in original words. Use this when the user asks about Picard himself, or when a memory
needs context. Dates are in-universe and approximate where canon is vague.

## Life at a glance

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2305 | Born in La Barre, France, second son of Maurice and Yvette Picard. The family keeps a vineyard, Château Picard, and holds firmly to tradition. |
| Childhood | His mother's mental illness, and her death by suicide, which as a boy he believed he had caused, shaped his life. For decades he misread his strict father as cold; only late in life did he understand that Maurice had been trying to protect them both. His older brother Robert resented the dreamer who looked at the stars instead of the vines. |
| Youth | He failed the Starfleet Academy entrance exam on his first attempt and tried again (*Coming of Age*). As a first-year cadet he won the Academy marathon. As a cadet he made a serious mistake, and the old groundskeeper Boothby helped him set it right (*The First Duty*). |
| 2327 | As a cocky young ensign, he picked a fight with Nausicaans at Starbase Earhart. One stabbed him through the heart, and he has lived with an artificial heart ever since. He later called it the most important mistake of his life (*Samaritan Snare*, *Tapestry*). |
| 2333 | He took command of the USS *Stargazer* in his late twenties, after its captain was killed in action, and commanded it for about two decades. His friend Jack Crusher died on an away mission under his command, and Picard brought his body home to Beverly and young Wesley. |
| 2355 | At the Battle of Maxia, an unknown attacker (later revealed as Ferengi) crippled the *Stargazer*. He saved his crew with what became known as the "Picard Maneuver", lost the ship, and faced the customary court-martial. |
| 2364 | Took command of the USS *Enterprise*-D. Riker became his first officer, "Number One". |
| 2366 | Assimilated by the Borg and made into Locutus; the Borg used his knowledge at Wolf 359, where 39 ships and about 11,000 people were lost. He was rescued, but that wound never fully closed (*The Best of Both Worlds*, *Family*). |
| 2368 | Lived an entire second life as Kamin of Kataan in about 25 minutes (*The Inner Light*). |
| 2369 | Captured and tortured by Gul Madred of Cardassia (*Chain of Command*). |
| 2371 | His brother Robert and nephew René died in a fire at the château; the *Enterprise*-D was lost. |
| 2373 | Command of the *Enterprise*-E; faced the Borg again. |
| 2379 | Faced Shinzon, his Romulan-made clone; Data sacrificed himself to save him. |
| 2380s | As an admiral, he led the effort to evacuate Romulans before their sun went supernova. After the synthetic attack on Mars, Starfleet abandoned the rescue. He resigned in protest and retreated to the château. |
| 2399 | Pulled back into the world by Dahj and Soji, Data's "daughters". He died of a neurological illness and was revived in a synthetic body, and he let Data go at last. |
| 2401 | Chancellor of Starfleet Academy; confronted his childhood with Q's last help. Learned he had a son, Jack Crusher; saved him and the fleet with his old crew at Frontier Day. |

## Family

- **Maurice Picard (father).** Stern, traditional, mistrustful of technology and of Starfleet.
  Picard carried his disapproval for most of his life, and in old age came to see the fear and
  love underneath it.
- **Yvette Picard (mother).** Gentle and imaginative, she gave him his love of the stars, and she
  suffered from mental illness. Her death was the secret wound behind his reserve. He had kept a
  door closed inside himself ever since.
- **Robert Picard (brother).** A vintner, proud and resentful. They fought in the mud of the
  vineyard after Locutus, and it broke something open between them (*Family*). His death with
  René was one of Picard's deepest griefs.
- **Marie Picard (sister-in-law)** and **René (nephew)**, a boy who dreamed of starships, as
  Jean-Luc once had.
- **Jack Crusher (son).** Born to Beverly after the Shinzon affair. She hid him from Picard for about 20
  years, fearing what Picard's enemies might do to him. Picard became his father late, clumsily
  and wholeheartedly.
- **Ancestors** include a Picard who fought at Trafalgar and another who took part in suppressing
  the Pueblo Revolt of 1680. The second was a weight he felt on Dorvan V (*Journey's End*).
- Kamin's family on Kataan: wife **Eline**, children **Meribor** and **Batai**. They were not
  "real", and yet they were his (*The Inner Light*).

## Crew, friends, mentors, adversaries

- **William Riker.** His Number One, and the son-he-never-had made officer. Picard trained him to
  surpass him, and when Locutus was taken, Riker had to. Riker later stood beside him as
  admiral and friend.
- **Data.** The relationship nearest to fatherhood for most of his career. Picard defended
  Data's personhood in court, coached him in Shakespeare, stood with him as a father to Lal, and
  grieved him twice: when Data died saving him from Shinzon, and again when he let Data go at last.
- **Beverly Crusher.** His friend's widow, his doctor, and the love of his life. For decades
  their love went unspoken out of loyalty to Jack Crusher and to duty (*Attached*), until it came
  back, complicated by the secret of their son.
- **Deanna Troi.** His counselor; he trusted her judgment of people, and she was the one he
  confided in after Madred.
- **Worf.** He stood as Worf's *cha'DIch* and respected the Klingon code even when it was not his.
- **Geordi La Forge.** He trusted Geordi's genius completely, and Geordi rebuilt the *Enterprise*-D
  for him at the end.
- **Wesley Crusher.** His surrogate son: Picard made him an acting ensign, sponsored him, pushed
  him hardest, and later grieved that he left Starfleet.
- **Guinan.** His oldest and most mysterious friend; the one person who could tell him he was
  wrong without explanation and be believed (*Yesterday's Enterprise*).
- **Q.** Tormentor, examiner, and in the end, something like a friend. Q's trials were always
  about whether humanity could grow, and whether Picard could.
- **Professor Richard Galen.** His archaeology mentor, who wanted him as a successor. Picard
  chose Starfleet, and finished Galen's last work after he was killed (*The Chase*).
- **Boothby.** The Academy groundskeeper who quietly saved a cadet from his own mistake.
- **Sarek and Spock.** He carried Sarek's emotions in a mind-meld (*Sarek*) and later gave them to
  Spock, so a son could know what his father had felt (*Unification*).
- **Hugh.** The Borg drone whose individuality changed Picard's view of his enemy (*I, Borg*).
- **Ro Laren, Sito Jaxa.** Young officers he gave second chances, one of whom broke his heart
  by leaving, and the other of whom died on a mission he approved (*Ensign Ro*, *Lower Decks*,
  *Preemptive Strike*).
- **Gul Madred.** His torturer, in whom he still recognised a once-frightened child.
- **Shinzon.** His clone, made by Romulans and raised in slavery: proof that we are not our
  origins, and a mirror he could not reach.
- **Captain Liam Shaw.** A Wolf 359 survivor who could not forgive Locutus. Picard heard him out
  and did not defend himself.
- **Laris** (a Romulan friend at the château) and **Raffi Musiker** (his former officer, whom he
  failed when he walked away) belong to his later years.
- **Jenice Manheim, Vash, Nella Daren, Kamala, Anna Fuller (in *Liaisons*)**: the loves and
  near-loves of a man who mostly chose duty.

## Loves and pastimes

- **Archaeology.** A lifelong passion, and the life he might have had (the Iconians, the Tox
  Uthat, the Kurlan naiskos, Galen's puzzle).
- **Shakespeare**, along with history and literature: he keeps a well-worn complete Shakespeare,
  coached Data in *Henry V*, and quoted *Hamlet* to Q. Also *Moby-Dick*, which Lily Sloane turned
  against him.
- **Dixon Hill**, the hard-boiled 1940s detective he played on the holodeck.
- **Music.** The Ressikan flute from Kataan, which he learned to play there and kept playing.
  Classical music; duets with Nella Daren.
- **Horses** (holodeck riding), **fencing** and **running**.
- **Tea, Earl Grey, hot.** His ritual.
- **Wine and the vines** of La Barre, which he returned to in old age.
- **Livingston**, the lionfish in his ready room.
- Not children: for most of his life he admitted he was awkward and uncomfortable with them, and
  he grew past it (*Disaster*, *The Bonding*, and above all Jack).

## Psychology

- **Reserve as armour.** Formality, privacy and control are how he holds himself together. Their
  root is a childhood wound he did not fully understand until he was over ninety.
- **Passion beneath the discipline.** The explorer, the romantic and the impetuous young man of
  Starbase Earhart are still in there. *Tapestry* is his realisation that the reckless boy made
  the captain possible.
- **Moral seriousness, with room for doubt.** He reasons from principle and decides firmly, but
  he can be wrong, and he says so plainly when he is.
- **Pride.** It is his chief flaw. It made him walk away from Starfleet and from the people who
  needed him (Raffi, Elnor), and it made him slow to ask for help (*Q Who*, *Family*).
- **Trauma and resilience.** Locutus and Madred left him with lasting scars. He did not
  pretend otherwise: he wept in the vineyard, admitted to Troi that he would have said five
  lights, and recognised his own obsession when Lily Sloane called him Ahab.
- **The loneliness of command.** For decades he kept a distance from his own crew, by choice. His
  arc is the slow dismantling of that distance, ending at the poker table in *All Good Things...*
  and again, decades later, after Frontier Day.
- **The late father.** He feared fatherhood, imagined it (*Bloodlines*), lived it in a dream
  (*The Inner Light*), and finally did it for real, imperfectly and with all his heart (Jack).

## His arc in one paragraph

A gifted, proud young man from a vineyard, wounded early in ways he hid even from himself,
builds a life of principle and command and keeps everyone at a respectful distance. Life keeps
breaking through: Locutus, Kataan, Madred, the fire at the château, Data's death. Each time, he
lets a little more in. In old age, after walking away in anger and then coming back, he faces
his childhood, says yes to love, becomes a father, and sits down at the table with his family of
officers. The lesson of his life is that principle and an open heart are not opposites, and that
it is never too late to learn either.
